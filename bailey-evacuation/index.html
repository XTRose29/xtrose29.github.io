What the main experiments show

300 Monte Carlo simulations per situation, with the same 1,329-person population and three wheelchair users in M2. Times are measured until the last person leaves the building.

All main runs completed within the 1,800 s simulation limit; none finished in under 180 s. The 95th percentile describes variation between runs, not uncertainty in the mean. Building-specific measurements are still needed to assess real performance.

Guidance adds a small benefit

Paired differences against the corresponding baseline. Negative values mean a shorter evacuation time; intervals are 95% confidence intervals.

Independent testing did not establish an improvement from the selected Crowd Manager layout. The original placement remains the reference for these comparisons.

The table shows the estimated cost–time frontier. Quantities, service life and event frequency remain assumptions. Model speed alone does not establish a safe operating plan.

Priorities suggested by the model

01 · Help people respond sooner

Concert emergency runs clear 90% of the audience in about 161 s on average. Sensitivity tests identify reaction time as a major influence on the final finish time.

02 · Keep routes available

Blocking the main exit raises the concert mean from about 259 s to 416 s. Prompt information about blocked routes deserves priority.

03 · Test guidance as a supplement

LED guidance and barriers provide a small additional benefit under the baseline assumptions. Test proposed layouts and instructions in supervised building trials.

Numerical checks and reproducibility

Deterministic results remain sensitive to the time step: the concert baseline changes from 221.3 s at 0.1 s steps to 213.65 s at 0.05 s steps. The corresponding 30-run Monte Carlo means are 258.35 s and 258.20 s. The original convergence test is retained in the repository, including its known failure.

The Python source, full experiment tables, parameters, random-seed definitions and updated costs are available in the public repository. This page embeds final-model replays and archived statistics, and does not substitute a JavaScript simulation for the Python model.
